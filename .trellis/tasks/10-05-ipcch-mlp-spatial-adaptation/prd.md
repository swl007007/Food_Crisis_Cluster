# IPCCH MLP spatial adaptation experiment

## Goal

Determine whether an MLP improves IPCCH population-share prediction and whether regional residual correction on existing maps adds value beyond matched pooled residual correction. Deliver a reproducible three-seed comparison and preserve negative results. Implementation and execution are reserved for Claude Opus 5.5 1M, with Codex supervision.

## Background and confirmed facts

- Previous IPCCH GeoXGB results did not establish a geographic increment over matched pooled predictions. This motivates separating learner performance from regional adaptation instead of assuming the partition is the sole cause.
- The existing contract defines q2/q3/q4/q5 population-share regression, bounded decreasing isotonic projection, an unrounded >=0.20 phase threshold, horizons 1/3/6/12 months, and a 36-month Stage3 window (`IPCCHGeoXGBExperiment/config/experiment-contract.json:5-35`).
- Local XGB models continue from immutable shared global roots, without ancestor-local accumulation (`IPCCHGeoXGBExperiment/config/experiment-contract.json:67`). Stage2 consensus is disabled (line 85).
- Current missing-value handling is XGBoost-native (line 35); an MLP needs an explicit fitting-only preprocessing contract.
- Existing FEWS NET/Ethiopia-specific guidance does not authorize importing its targets, horizons, SMOTE, or validation policies into IPCCH.
- Read-only code inspection found no executable NN/MLP backend in current `src/model/` or `app/`: neural imports are commented (`src/model/GeoRF.py:16`), and the current adapters are GF/XGB/DT (`src/model/adapters.py:55,70,94`). Historical neural configuration names are not a working implementation. This finding does not cover external repositories or historical versions.
- The IPCCH quartet explicitly requires each local scalar learner to inherit its own global target model and rejects local-to-local ancestry (`IPCCHGeoXGBExperiment/ipcch_geoxgb/quartet.py:258-278`). Generic RF/legacy XGB load/train helpers do not establish neural fine-tuning semantics.

## Requirements

- R1: Separate pooled MLP versus pooled XGB from regional MLP versus its own matched pooled model. Do not attribute overall learner improvement to geography.
- R2: Resolve maps, local adaptation, calendar, preprocessing, selection budget, randomness, evaluation, and stopping rules before execution.
- R3: Preserve previous packages and runs; isolate approved scientific changes.
- R4: Produce a converged PRD, design, implementation plan, and Claude handoff after grilling; verify executor identity and requested model before release.
- R5: Freeze the selected existing map membership throughout the experiment. Compare pooled and regional MLP without making pooled MLP superiority over XGB a prerequisite for examining regional effects. A negative result applies to this XGB-selected map, not all MLP-specific partitions.
- R6: Use the original P6 maps from `p6-formal-20261004b`, learned through 2022 and covering 3,264 learning areas per horizon. Retain H1/H3/H6/H12 and the original 2023–2025 main / 2026 supplementary evaluation schedules, including horizon-specific first targets and missing-truth folds. Exclude the split2024 maps from this experiment. Treat results as exploratory because the evaluation periods have already been inspected.
- R7: Use four independent scalar MLP regressors, one for each of q2/q3/q4/q5, rather than a shared multi-output network. Retain the existing bounded decreasing isotonic projection and unrounded >=0.20 phase decoding; no cross-target parameter sharing.
- R8: Freeze the fitted global scalar MLP and train a separate regional residual network for the matching target. Form raw regional predictions as global prediction plus residual-network output, then jointly project the four cumulative shares and decode. Do not fine-tune global weights, share target parameters, or accumulate corrections from other regions/ancestors. Residual construction and input source are specified in R10-R11.
- R9: Include three MLP arms: base pooled global; frozen global plus a pooled residual network; frozen global plus regional residual networks. Pooled and regional residual networks use the same architecture and training recipe, with each correcting the same global target model rather than stacking the regional correction on the pooled residual correction. Preserve the base global arm to measure the effect of adding correction capacity. This comparator is not exact total-parameter-budget matching because the regional system has multiple residual networks. Deployment gate reference/fallback follows R13.
- R10: Use in-sample residual targets on the global model's lawful fitting rows: r_q = y_q - g_q(X), with raw unprojected global predictions. Fit the pooled residual network on the full fitting pool and each regional residual network on its regional subset. Independent validation/test targets must not enter either fitting step. This is sequential boosting-style residual fitting, not temporal out-of-fold stacking; do not claim residual targets are out-of-sample errors. Global overfit may suppress the training residual signal and remains a limitation to report.
- R11: Feed global, pooled-residual, and regional-residual networks the same original rich561 predictor source after fitting-only preprocessing. Do not substitute global hidden representations or add predicted-share inputs. True residuals are supervised targets only, never prediction-time features. Missing-value representation and scaling follow R12.
- R12: Fit per-feature medians and standardization on the lawful global fitting pool only. Compute value-column means/scales after median imputation; fill training-all-missing columns with 0 and use scale 1 for zero-variance columns, without dropping columns. Append one binary, unscaled missingness flag per original predictor in a fixed order: 561 values plus 561 flags, totaling 1,122 inputs. All four targets and pooled/regional residual networks associated with that global fit share the frozen transform. Never refit it on regional, validation, or test rows. This preserves missingness information but remains a learner-specific encoding change relative to XGB native NaN handling.
- R13: Recompute Stage3 gate decisions from MLP historical predictions on identical lawful keys. Compare global-plus-regional-residual against global-plus-pooled-residual, retaining the original support conditions and strict projected/decoded crisis-F1 gain >0.01. Unmapped, statistically unsupported, and gain-rejected cases use global-plus-pooled-residual. Do not reuse XGB enabled flags. Keep base global separately reported; do not add a gate choosing base versus pooled residual. The primary geographic contrast is the routed regional system versus the pooled residual system. Neither pooled residual superiority over base global nor regional gains are assumed. Technical fit/prediction failures must not be silently classified as statistical fallback.
- R14: Limit architecture selection to a predeclared grid of two global-network sizes and two residual-network sizes, yielding four combinations. Use only development data through 2022 for selection and freeze the selected recipe before Stage3. Pooled and regional residual arms share the candidate residual architecture. Do not conduct independent architecture searches for each region or each target. Sizes and selection follow R16 and R15 respectively.
- R15: For each horizon, select one global/residual architecture recipe using the global-plus-pooled-residual arm's projected/decoded crisis F1 on the original complete common development S, aggregating TP/FP/FN across keys rather than averaging regional F1. Apply that selected recipe to all four independent targets and all regions for the horizon. Retain the corresponding base global model as the matched reference, not an independently optimized global arm. Do not select by regional uplift. This may favor capacity appropriate to pooled fitting rather than the best regional architecture; disclose that limit. Seed aggregation follows R20; deterministic tie-breaking must be specified in the design.
- R16: Use global hidden widths (64,32) or (128,64), and residual hidden width (16,) or (32,), with ReLU hidden activations and a linear scalar output throughout. For 1,122 inputs with biases, global parameter counts are 73,985 / 152,065 and residual counts are 17,985 / 35,969 per target network. Initialize the final output-layer weight and bias of both pooled and regional residual networks to zero, so both start at zero correction. Retain PyTorch Linear default initialization for their hidden layers and all global-network layers; never zero-initialize the whole network. Keep residual outputs signed; do not clip individual network predictions before addition and joint projection. These are predeclared candidates, not empirically established optimal sizes. Do not add unapproved layers or representation modules.
- R17: Train each global network for exactly 100 epochs and each pooled/regional residual network for exactly 40 epochs; use final-epoch weights. Do not apply validation-based early stopping, select a best epoch, or alter budgets based on S, gate, or test scores. Each epoch visits all lawful fitting rows once; equal epochs across different pool sizes do not imply equal optimizer-step counts. Record actual update counts and training-loss histories. L−P compares these fixed training procedures, not an isolated geographic effect at equal optimization budgets; fewer local steps alone do not establish undertraining. Zero initialization does not guarantee convergence or prevent overfit. Non-finite training/predictions are technical failures, not an invitation to tune or silently return a fallback.
- R18: Use unweighted mean squared error on raw scalar shares/residuals and AdamW with constant learning rate 0.001, betas (0.9,0.999), epsilon 1e-8. Use batch size min(256,n_fit), shuffle legal fitting rows each epoch with recorded RNG, and retain final partial batches. Do not add batch normalization, a learning-rate schedule, or target standardization. Apply the same recipe to global, pooled-residual, and regional-residual learners without additional parameter search. Crisis F1 remains the selection/gate metric, not the gradient-training objective. Regularization follows R19, which supersedes the earlier weight decay 0.0001 / no-dropout choice.
- R19: Apply dropout p=0.10 after every hidden ReLU, not on inputs or scalar outputs, and AdamW weight decay 0.01 on weight matrices only, excluding biases. Use these settings for global, pooled-residual, and regional-residual networks, without adding search dimensions or changing the fixed epoch budgets. Generate all frozen-global residual targets in evaluation mode with dropout disabled; also disable dropout for validation, historical gate, and test predictions. Keep the frozen global network in evaluation mode while fitting residual networks. Stronger regularization does not guarantee generalization; equal decay coefficients with different optimizer-step counts do not imply equal total shrinkage.
- R20: Use three predeclared replicate seeds, 42/43/44, for development and full Stage3 evaluation. Select each horizon's one shared architecture recipe by the arithmetic mean of the three per-seed pooled-residual development S crisis-F1 scores. Run that recipe under all three seeds, recomputing gate decisions separately per replicate. Report per-seed metrics and matched geographic deltas plus descriptive mean/range. Do not select the best seed or average predictions into an ensemble; the three replicates are not three independent datasets and do not exhaust training uncertainty.
- R21: Additionally fit/predict current-date regional corrections for all mapped regions with current evaluation keys and sufficient original current-fitting support, regardless of historical gate acceptance. Report ungated regional versus pooled-residual performance on exactly the same supported keys, with eligible counts and gate-rejection reasons. Keep G−P as the primary full-cohort deployment contrast and L−P as the supported-cohort diagnostic. Predeclare historical-support eligible main-period counts of 3,211/4,997/3,423/845 for H1/H3/H6/H12 (18.54%/29.53%/20.86%/6.00% of their full cohorts), and recompute them from keys/support rules before fitting. These upper bounds on rows that can change through regional routing are not bounds on F1 gain or significance; current fitting support and gain acceptance may reduce coverage further. This diagnostic cannot select recipes, relax gate thresholds, or be described as an oracle. Do not extrapolate its subset effect to all keys. Include additional current-local fits in the pre-run computation inventory and reuse exact matching models where available.
- R22: Preserve the previously requested full metric panel and persistence comparison: four-class accuracy/macro-F1, binary accuracy/F1/precision/recall/F2, projected q3 R² with raw q3 R² as a diagnostic. Retain original NA, matched-cohort, availability and country-bootstrap semantics as detailed in design section 8. Report each seed and horizon, not a cross-horizon headline or selected best seed; keep 2026 separate.
- R23: Deliver keyed predictions, full fit/parent/preprocessing provenance, per-candidate selection records, all gate decisions, diagnostic eligibility, and saved-model replay. ## Out of scope and limitations

MLP-driven map relearning, shared multi-output networks, global-weight fine-tuning, K-means/exchange, learned edges, GNN, SMOTE, gate relaxation, early stopping, new seed ensembles and broad hyperparameter search are excluded. There is no new raw-data version, release-vintage reconstruction or donor assignment. The XGB-selected map, reused S and already viewed test period limit interpretation. Pooled recipe selection may not be best for small regional samples; fixed training budgets and stronger regularization do not guarantee convergence or generalization.

PyTorch is not installed in the inspected original Windows or current WSL runtime. A compatible official wheel exists, but actual import, deterministic behavior, CPU/GPU performance and full-run duration remain P0 operational checks. They do not authorize changing model size, seed count or numerical recipe to obtain a favorable result. No implementation or audit lifecycle action has been performed during planning.
